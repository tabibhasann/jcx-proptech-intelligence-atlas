## 1. The ten-minute crash course

### The one-sentence thesis

JCX does not only need a newer website. It may need a connected digital operating model in which project facts, leads, sales, finance, construction, handover, service and future building operations use reliable information and accountable workflows.

### The five lessons to remember

1. **A beautiful interface is only the front door.** If project information is duplicated, lead routing is unclear, and construction updates are not connected to an approved source, a redesign can make the surface better while leaving the business problem intact.
2. **Data and ownership come before AI.** Automation needs a reliable record, a named owner, clear permissions, an exception path and a way to measure whether the result helped.
3. **The best real-estate companies connect workflows.** Aldar, Emaar, CapitaLand, JLL, Prologis and others use technology across customer journeys, capital, project delivery, procurement, service, energy or portfolio operations. They are not simply adding more apps.
4. **A bounded pilot is more credible than a giant promise.** Start with one painful process, one accountable owner, one project or cluster, a baseline and a stop-or-scale decision.
5. **PropTech is broader than property listings.** It includes land and title, feasibility, BIM, construction, procurement, CRM, finance, handover, facilities, energy, climate, data and governance.

### The current JCX hypothesis

Public evidence suggests that JCX is a fast-growing Bangladeshi developer with residential, commercial and condominium activity, a Japanese collaboration narrative through Creed Group, active handovers and launches, and several housing-finance relationships. Its public values include trust, customer happiness, quality, integrity, timely delivery and green living.

The strongest digital signal is a recent IT/MIS recruitment notice describing organization-wide ERP implementation, integration, data migration, BI/MIS, cybersecurity, disaster recovery, training and adoption across functions such as finance, sales, supply chain, customer service, CRM and construction. A 2024 public post mentions an Odoo-related ERP meeting. Treat Odoo as a clue, not a confirmed fact.

The likely strategic question is therefore:

> How can JCX turn a growing portfolio and active operation into a reliable, measurable digital system that improves trust, sales, delivery visibility and internal control?

That is a hypothesis. The meeting is where you test it.

### The recommended first move

> Build a governed digital front door connected to CRM and ERP, digitize the highest-friction workflows in phases, then run one measured smart-asset pilot before scaling.

For the first commercial pilot, the leading hypothesis is a **Jolshiri/Jalshiri lead-to-booking spine**, after JCX confirms the canonical spelling and priority. It would connect campaign/source, enquiry, response, qualification, site visit, approved project and unit information, finance referral, booking and follow-up. Do not present this as a final answer. Present it as a testable hypothesis.

### Your opening

> “Thank you for making the time. I spent some time reviewing JCX’s public portfolio, recent handovers, financing partnerships, and the ERP and IT/MIS direction. I can see that the website sits inside a much broader operating model involving sales, project status, financing, landowners, construction, and customer trust. I have a few initial observations, but I would prefer to validate them with your team before suggesting any solution. Could we begin with what prompted this initiative now and what leadership most wants to change?”

### Your positioning in thirty seconds

> “We help connect digital initiatives to measurable outcomes: better-qualified demand, faster response, more reliable project and customer information, clearer reporting, and less operational friction. A new website may be part of that, but we would design it around the journeys, data, ownership, and integrations that make it valuable.”

### The one example to use if time is short

Use **Mapletree** when the conversation turns to value measurement:

> “Mapletree’s energy analytics case distinguishes about £88,000 of opportunity identified from about £65,000 already realized through completed actions. That distinction is important. A dashboard or AI recommendation is not the same thing as a completed, measured business result. JCX should use the same discipline for any pilot.”

The figures are from a company case study. They are not an independent audit and are not a promise of JCX savings.

### The six things you need before leaving

- The business outcome leadership wants.
- The highest-friction journey or process.
- The system of record for project, inventory, price, lead, customer and status data.
- The ERP/CRM platform, implementation stage and responsible owners.
- A bounded diagnostic or pilot with inputs and decision criteria.
- A named next action, owner and date.

### Why the business context matters

Public interviews also describe pressure in the wider sector and in management’s view of the market: weaker demand for large luxury units, slower instalments, higher material costs, higher home-loan rates, longer approval cycles, unsold inventory and cash-flow pressure. These are not audited JCX financial results.

The digital implications are practical:

- Qualified demand matters more than raw enquiry volume.
- A buyer should be matched to location, size, price band, availability policy and finance path.
- A late response can lose a high-value opportunity.
- Leadership needs to know which channel, campaign, partner and salesperson produce site visits and bookings.
- Project, unit, price and construction facts must remain consistent across website, sales, finance and internal systems.
- Reporting should reduce manual reconciliation rather than add another dashboard.
- Long sales, construction, payment and handover cycles require visible, trusted updates.

### What you do not know yet

Do not pretend the public record answers these questions:

- Which ERP and CRM are actually in production?
- Which system owns project status, inventory, price, customer, payment and construction data?
- Who approves public project facts and how often?
- Where website, phone, WhatsApp, broker and social leads go?
- How quickly a person responds to a lead?
- Which digital project is already committed, budgeted or contracted?
- What budget, deadline, procurement route and vendor constraints exist?
- What the customer and staff experience is internally?

These unknowns are not a weakness in your preparation. They are the reason for a discovery meeting.

### The evidence ladder

The research uses source quality and claim attribution separately.

**Source grades:**

- **S1:** filing, regulator, standard, signed customer or authoritative documentation.
- **S2:** company website, press release, vendor/customer case study or accelerator profile.
- **S3:** reputable independent research or reporting with a clear method.
- **S4:** structured directory or database requiring verification.
- **S5:** social, review or listicle signal used mainly for discovery.

When speaking, use verbs that match the evidence:

- “The filing reports…”
- “The company says…”
- “A customer case study describes…”
- “Independent reporting suggests…”
- “The public signal is…”
- “Our working inference is…”
- “This remains to be validated.”

Never turn a company announcement into an audited outcome. Never turn funding, a logo, accelerator membership or an AI label into proof that a product works.

### The two-dimensional map

Every company should be understood by both **where it acts in the property lifecycle** and **how it creates value**.

**Lifecycle, in plain language:**

1. Land, site and origination.
2. Feasibility and development strategy.
3. Capital, finance and transactions.
4. Design, BIM and preconstruction.
5. Construction delivery.
6. Marketing, sales, leasing and distribution.
7. Occupancy and customer experience.
8. Property and facility operations.
9. Asset, portfolio and investment management.
10. ESG, climate, resilience and health.
11. Handover, warranty and end-of-life.
12. Cross-lifecycle data and trust.

**Technology layers:**

1. Systems of record such as ERP, CRM, accounting, project controls and documents.
2. Applications and bounded workflows such as inspections, visits, RFQs and service.
3. Data and intelligence such as analytics, geospatial, simulation and AI.
4. Physical and connected assets such as sensors, meters, BMS, cameras and robotics.
5. Spatial visualization such as BIM viewers, digital twins, virtual tours and reality capture.
6. Market and financial rails such as marketplaces, mortgage, payments, signature and identity.
7. Infrastructure and governance such as APIs, security, privacy, standards and assurance.

“AI,” “digital twin,” “IoT” and “blockchain” are mechanisms. They are not complete business cases. Always ask: who uses it, which workflow changes, what data is needed, what decision or action follows, and how the result is measured.

### Global examples you can explain

#### Aldar: the connected developer pattern

Aldar is useful because it treats digital sales, customer experience, digital agreements, procurement and smart assets as connected parts of the business. Its public reports describe a Digital Spine and company-reported adoption and efficiency results.

**What to say:**

> “Aldar is a useful example of a developer treating digital as an operating model, not just a marketing site. The transferable lesson is the connection between customer, commercial, procurement and data workflows. The reported numbers are company-reported and should not be copied as a JCX forecast.”

#### Emaar: a portal earns adoption through real tasks

Emaar One is valuable as a customer-experience benchmark because it supports payments, service and property interactions. Emaar’s 2023 and 2024 reports describe tens of thousands of users, payment activity, service closure and customer-satisfaction metrics.

**What to say:**

> “A customer portal earns adoption by helping people complete real tasks such as payments, documents and service requests. A portal that only displays marketing content will not create the same habit.”

#### CapitaLand: disciplined building pilots

CapitaLand’s sustainability challenge and building pilots show a useful pattern: one physical system, one baseline, an owner, an intervention and a measured result. Examples include AI cooling optimization, automated sprinkler testing and cooling-tower water reduction.

**What to say:**

> “The important part is not that the technology sounds advanced. It is that the pilot has a bounded site, a measurable baseline and a decision about whether to scale.”

#### JLL: distribution and data matter

JLL combines service distribution, workflow products, internal AI adoption and venture activity. It is useful to understand why a real-estate company with many clients, buildings and employees can develop a data advantage.

**What to say:**

> “The advantage is not only the model. It is the combination of data, embedded workflow, users and distribution. JCX should first create that internal operating discipline before trying to become a platform.”

#### Prologis: sell the outcome, not the device

Prologis connects logistics assets, energy, automation, procurement, financing and vendor coordination. Some public cases report realized lighting savings, while other figures are modeled or expected.

**What to say:**

> “A technology partner should be judged by the business outcome and implementation responsibility, not by the number of devices or the novelty of the demo.”

#### Mitsui Fudosan: relevance to the Japanese relationship

Mitsui Fudosan is a useful Japanese reference for customer networks, neighborhood platforms, digital twins and operational experimentation. Keep the analogy at the operating-model level. Do not claim that a Japanese program transfers directly to Bangladesh.

#### Lendlease: construction information has to survive the lifecycle

Lendlease links design, BIM, field capture, delivery and operations. The lesson is that a 3D model is not automatically a digital twin. It becomes valuable when its information is versioned, connected to work and reused after handover.

## 5. What the research actually proves

### Adoption is broad, scaled value is rare

JLL’s 2025 global technology survey reports 92% of corporate real-estate teams running or planning AI pilots, but only 5% achieving most program goals. Its 2026 outlook reports that many investors lack a unified technology strategy, many occupiers lack an AI change framework, and many organizations lack sufficient digital talent.

The useful conclusion is not that AI is bad. It is that enthusiasm is not the same as operational value.

### Construction is less digitized than product demos suggest

The EIB Investment Report 2025/26 reports moderate 3D BIM adoption and very low 4D BIM adoption in the European construction sample it cites. Cost, skills, fragmentation, interoperability and inconsistent client demand remain obstacles.

For JCX, digital construction requires standards, contractor onboarding, field-friendly workflows, version control, export and adoption. A vendor demo is not proof that the whole delivery chain is digitized.

### Five outcome stories to remember

| Case | What happened | How to interpret it |
|---|---|---|
| Mapletree | About £88,000 identified and about £65,000 reported as realized through energy actions. | Track identified opportunity separately from completed and verified value. |
| Aldar | Reports digital visits, customer usage, digital signatures and workflow-efficiency improvements. | A connected developer operating model can make digital adoption visible, but the metrics remain company-reported. |
| Emaar One | Tens of thousands of users, payments, service closure and satisfaction metrics in annual reports. | Portals need real tasks, not only content. |
| CapitaLand | Building-specific cooling, sprinkler and cooling-tower pilots with reported measurable results. | Start with a bounded pilot, baseline and owner. |
| Digital Realty | ENERGY STAR profile reports energy savings over a dated period. | Energy claims need a defined boundary, period and normalization. |

### What the failure cases teach

- **Zillow Offers:** forecasting and data did not remove inventory, pricing and balance-sheet risk.
- **WeWork:** a digital customer experience could not repair lease duration, utilization and capital-structure exposure.
- **Katerra:** vertical integration multiplied factory, working-capital, quality and execution risk.
- **View:** technically differentiated building hardware still faced commercialization and capital risk.
- **Sidewalk Labs/Quayside:** legitimacy, public purpose, data governance and scope are product requirements.
- **Veev:** startup survival and technology survival are different. A technology can continue under a strategic owner after a venture structure fails.

Use these examples only when useful. They show maturity and balance. They are not meant to frighten JCX away from innovation.

## 6. What JCX could build, in stages

### The JCX Digital Spine in plain language

Think of five connected layers:

1. **Digital front door:** fast bilingual website, project finder, relevant content, correct enquiry forms, finance path and trusted status information.
2. **Commercial spine:** every enquiry enters CRM with project, source, campaign, intent, consent, assigned owner, response deadline and lifecycle status.
3. **Operational spine:** ERP, CRM, project controls, procurement, finance, construction evidence, documents and customer service exchange controlled information rather than duplicate it.
4. **Customer and partner spine:** buyer, landowner, broker and customer portals for documents, progress, payments, appointments, handover, defects and service.
5. **Intelligence spine:** governed data, leadership dashboards and bounded AI with human approval, audit and correction.

### What the website should own and what it should not

The website should own public editorial presentation, search, content workflow and experience. It should consume approved project and commercial records.

The website should not become a parallel ERP. It should not independently own live inventory, receivables, customer contracts, construction controls or payment truth.

Every public project record should have:

- stable project ID and official name;
- canonical location and map coordinates;
- property type, audience and lifecycle stage;
- approved status and disclosure language;
- unit-size range, configuration, parking, plot or floor information where relevant;
- price or availability policy with an effective date if published;
- construction and handover milestones;
- approved media, plans, brochures, certificates and partner evidence;
- content owner, data owner, approver, last verified date and review date;
- CRM, ERP, project-control and document identifiers where integrated.

### The minimum lead record

Collect only what JCX will use and protect:

- project, location and intent;
- name and verified contact method;
- preferred language and channel;
- buyer, landowner, investor, broker or existing-customer journey;
- size, budget and timing only when justified;
- source, campaign, UTM, landing page and partner;
- consent purpose, notice version, timestamp and preferences;
- duplicate result, assigned owner and response deadline;
- appointment, qualification and later booking outcome where authorized.

### The phased roadmap

#### Phase 0, align and baseline, approximately 2 to 4 weeks

Interview leadership, sales, marketing, IT/MIS, ERP owners, construction, finance, customer service and current vendors. Map systems, spreadsheets, data owners, privacy constraints and priority journeys. Establish baseline metrics before promising improvements.

**Gate:** named sponsor, process owner, data owner, agreed problem and one pilot choice.

#### Phase 1, digital front door and lead spine, 0 to 90 days after alignment

Create the governed project catalogue, audience-specific journeys, correct forms, CRM routing, response SLA, attribution, mobile performance and security baseline. Keep the visual ambition high, but make accuracy and measurability non-negotiable.

**Gate:** field mapping, event taxonomy, integration and export tested; staff can use the workflow.

#### Phase 2, commercial and project workflows, 3 to 6 months

Connect inventory and booking where appropriate, finance referrals, procurement, project controls, progress evidence, collections and management reporting.

**Gate:** role controls, audit trail, reconciliation and contractor adoption pass.

#### Phase 3, customer and landowner self-service, 6 to 12 months

Launch customer progress, documents, payments, appointments, handover, defects and service. Give landowners secure document and milestone visibility.

**Gate:** completeness, service-level and satisfaction outcomes are measurable.

#### Phase 4, smart assets and advanced intelligence, 9 to 18 months

Select one operational asset with meters, BMS data, bills, a facility owner and a clear use case. Link insights to work orders. Scale only after realized value is measured.

### Build, buy, partner or invest

- **Buy or configure** mature non-differentiating workflows such as CRM, CDE, identity, CMMS and analytics.
- **Build** local strategic layers such as bilingual portals, approved project records, landowner/JV workflows and Bangladesh-specific payment or legal integrations.
- **Partner** for specialist data, BIM, geospatial, title evidence, BMS, construction vision or hardware.
- **Run a paid venture-client pilot** when one startup can solve one measurable problem in 8 to 12 weeks.
- **Invest separately** only after a product has repeatable production value, strategic fit and independent investment merit. A pilot should not require JCX to invest.

## 7. Leading the first meeting

### The posture

Your strongest position is not “I know JCX better than JCX.” It is “I prepared seriously, I can see how the pieces connect, and I know which assumptions need to be checked.”

Use phrases such as:

- “I observed…”
- “The public evidence suggests…”
- “This may create…”
- “I could not verify…”
- “How does this work internally?”
- “What is the authoritative source?”
- “Who owns the outcome?”
- “What would we measure before and after?”

Listen more than you speak. Do not present a parade of 44 companies. Use one example that matches the client’s problem.

### A practical 60-minute structure

| Time | Focus |
|---:|---|
| 0 to 7 minutes | Introductions, context, desired outcome and opening. |
| 7 to 17 | Why now, leadership goal, deadline and decisions already made. |
| 17 to 40 | Customer journeys, ERP/CRM, data, ownership, operations and measurement. |
| 40 to 48 | Three or four observations and one relevant benchmark. |
| 48 to 55 | Stakeholders, vendors, constraints, budget and pilot. |
| 55 to 60 | Read back what you heard, correct assumptions and agree next action. |

### The first twelve questions

1. “What happened or what opportunity appeared that made this a priority now?”
2. “If this succeeds in twelve months, what will leadership see changing?”
3. “Which journey creates the most friction today: buyer lead to booking, landowner, project reporting, procurement, construction, collections, handover or service?”
4. “What do buyers and landowners repeatedly ask, wait for or complain about?”
5. “Which system is the source of truth for project status, inventory, price, approval, customer and payment data?”
6. “I saw public evidence of an ERP program and an earlier Odoo-related signal. What platform and modules are selected, and where is the implementation now?”
7. “When a website, phone, WhatsApp, broker, social or bank lead arrives, who owns it and how fast should a human respond?”
8. “How are project facts approved once and kept consistent across the website, brochure, sales, finance and ERP?”
9. “Which management reports are trusted today, and which still require manual collection?”
10. “What security, privacy, legal, hosting, integration or current-vendor constraints are fixed?”
11. “Who is executive sponsor, daily business owner, technical owner and final approver?”
12. “Which one project or process could prove measurable value within one quarter?”

### Questions by stakeholder

**Leadership and strategy**

- What business problem matters more than the website itself?
- Is the ambition to digitize JCX, invest in external PropTech, or both?
- What cannot be compromised: trust, quality, delivery, margin, cash flow, speed or customer experience?
- Which decision does leadership currently make too slowly or with too little visibility?

**Sales and marketing**

- What is the current funnel from enquiry to contact, qualification, visit, booking and cancellation?
- What is the median and 90th-percentile first-response time?
- How are broker and bank referrals attributed?
- What information does a salesperson need before calling a lead?
- Which channels produce qualified business rather than only traffic?

**IT/MIS and ERP**

- What is the ERP platform, implementation partner, module scope and go-live sequence?
- Is CRM inside the ERP or separate?
- Which integrations are live, planned or blocked?
- How are identity, access, backup, disaster recovery, monitoring, patching and vendor exit handled?
- What data migration and adoption risks are already known?

**Construction and projects**

- How are schedule, cost, RFI, quality, progress, change and photo evidence recorded?
- Which project or model identifier connects design, procurement, site and handover?
- Can field teams work in low-connectivity conditions?
- Who approves a progress update before customers see it?

**Finance and procurement**

- How do requisition, quotation, approval, purchase order, delivery, invoice and payment connect?
- Where do unmatched payments or manual reconciliations occur?
- What is the current collections and instalment visibility?
- Which category would be a safe procurement pilot?

**Customer service and handover**

- What questions create the most repeat calls?
- How are appointments, documents, defects, warranty and service tickets tracked?
- What must a customer be able to do without calling?
- What evidence would make a handover experience feel trustworthy?

### Three respectful website observations

Use no more than three or four. Ask a validation question after each.

**Buyer form**

> “The buyer journey appears to reuse fields intended for landowners, including land size and a landowner phone label. That may be a simple template issue, but it could affect buyer confidence and the data your team receives. Where do these submissions land, and how are the fields mapped?”

**Construction status**

> “Construction status could become one of JCX’s strongest trust features. In my public test, it did not provide usable type or location selections. What internal source is intended to power it, and who owns update frequency and approval?”

**Project data**

> “I saw a few project attributes represented differently across public components, including apartment-size and location naming differences. With a portfolio of this scale, manual duplication naturally becomes difficult. Which system owns the approved project record?”

**Mobile and measurement**

> “Desktop is visually strong and performed well in the lab. The simulated mobile path was slower, and the public analytics setup does not let me verify whether call, WhatsApp, form success, visit, qualified lead, booking and revenue events are joined end to end. What do field data and CRM reporting show?”

### A three-minute insight segment

Use this after you have listened:

> “I saw four things worth validating. First, the site already has a premium visual foundation, active content and substantial portfolio material. Second, the buyer form appears to reuse landowner fields, which may affect both conversion and lead data. Third, the construction-status experience did not provide usable choices in my test, even though progress transparency could be one of JCX’s strongest trust tools. Fourth, several project facts appear in multiple forms, which suggests the site needs a governed project-data source. The lab results also show a desktop-mobile gap. None of this tells me the internal cause, so I would want to map it with sales, marketing, IT, ERP, construction, finance and customer service before recommending a rebuild.”

Then ask:

> “Which of these is closest to the problem leadership wants solved?”

### The close

> “My current understanding is that the highest-value opportunity may be connecting project and customer information more reliably across the website, sales, financing, construction, service and ERP, not simply changing the visual design. I would like to confirm the source of truth, accountable owner, priority journey and success measures before recommending a solution. Would a short diagnostic or one-project pilot be the right next decision?”

## 8. Explain the difficult concepts simply

### ERP

An ERP is the organization’s core transaction and control system. It may cover finance, procurement, inventory, projects, HR, supply chain and other modules. It is not automatically the best public website or customer experience layer.

**Say:** “The important question is not only which ERP is selected. It is which records and workflows it owns, how the website and CRM connect, and how errors and changes are handled.”

### CRM

A CRM manages relationships and commercial activity: leads, opportunities, calls, visits, follow-up, channel partners, customer service and sometimes marketing.

**Say:** “The website should create a useful lead record, not merely send an email notification.”

### Master data and source of truth

Master data is the agreed record for a project, unit, customer, vendor, asset or location. A source of truth is the system and ownership model that decides which value is authoritative.

**Say:** “If Olympus has two apartment-size ranges in public pages, the technical problem is not only a typo. We need one approved project record, an owner and a publishing workflow.”

### Digital twin

A digital twin is a maintained digital representation of a real asset, process or system that supports a decision or action. A 3D model or dashboard alone is not automatically a digital twin.

**Say:** “A useful twin would tell a named operator something actionable, such as which equipment needs attention, and connect that insight to a work order.”

### AI agent

An AI agent is software that can interpret information and perform a bounded sequence of actions. The safe version has approved data, permissions, human escalation, logging, evaluation and a kill switch.

**Say:** “AI is strong for approved knowledge search, document classification, report drafts and exception triage. It should not invent price, title, finance eligibility or construction status.”

### Venture client

A venture client is a real customer that gives a startup a paid, bounded problem, users, data, baseline and production decision. It is often more useful to JCX than becoming an investor immediately.

**Say:** “First become a demanding customer. If the product works across multiple projects and has a broader market, then consider investment separately.”

### Identified, forecast and realized value

- **Identified:** the analysis suggests an opportunity.
- **Forecast or modeled:** a projection based on assumptions.
- **Target:** the result the team intends to achieve.
- **Realized:** the result observed after implementation.
- **Verified:** the realized result checked against an agreed method and baseline.

Never mix these categories.

## 9. Measurement, governance and risk

### Baseline before target

For every initiative define:

- the exact process start and end;
- representative baseline period and seasonality;
- volume;
- current time, conversion, error, cost, delay, satisfaction or risk;
- intervention and expected mechanism;
- target and time horizon;
- evidence type;
- owner and data source;
- build, license, integration, training, support and change cost.

### Website and commercial metrics

- Form completion and valid-submission rate.
- Percentage of leads with project, source, campaign, intent and consent.
- Duplicate and invalid-lead rate.
- Median and 90th-percentile human response time.
- Contact, qualification, appointment, site-visit and booking rates.
- Cost per qualified lead, visit and booking.
- Project-data defect rate and freshness.
- Mobile Core Web Vitals from field data.
- Publishing and approval cycle time.
- Form and integration failure rate.

### Operational metrics

- Project report timeliness.
- Schedule and cost forecast accuracy.
- RFI, NCR, defect and variation closure time.
- Requisition-to-PO time and invoice exceptions.
- Bank reconciliation and month-end close duration.
- On-time instalment and unmatched-payment rates.
- Portal adoption and self-service completion.
- Handover cycle, defect closure and service response.
- Preventive-maintenance completion and energy intensity.
- ERP active use and manual-work reduction.

### Security and privacy questions

The public audit sampled a response exposing PHP 7.4.33, an end-of-life branch, and found several common security headers absent from that response. This does not prove a breach. Ask who owns hosting, patching, WAF, backups, restore testing, monitoring, incident response and upgrade planning.

The privacy policy and public tracking setup should be reconciled with the actual data flows. Ask legal and IT to define purpose, consent, retention, vendor access, deletion, correction and security requirements, especially for NID/passport files, payments, CCTV, biometrics or precise occupancy data.

### Non-negotiable system and vendor questions

- Can all content, records, attachments, metadata and audit history be exported?
- Are stable external IDs available?
- Are APIs, webhooks, bulk export and error semantics documented?
- Are SSO, MFA, role-based access, encryption, retention and subprocessors clear?
- Is there a sandbox and a tested backup/restore process?
- Can field users work with weak connectivity?
- What are the RPO, RTO, support and incident-notification commitments?
- Can JCX leave without losing its data or history?

## 10. Objections and mature replies

**“We only need a website.”**

> “We can keep the delivery scoped to the website. I would still map project data, lead routing, measurement, privacy and ERP/CRM touchpoints so the new site does not become another isolated system.”

**“We already have an agency.”**

> “That can be an advantage. We can support the current team with diagnosis, requirements, integration and data design, measurement or a bounded workstream rather than assuming replacement is necessary.”

**“Just send a proposal.”**

> “I can send a responsible proposal after confirming the outcome, owner, systems, scope boundary, timing and commercial range. Otherwise it would be a guess. A fixed-output discovery proposal may be the right first document.”

**“Can AI automate everything?”**

> “AI can remove repetitive work in approved, measurable workflows. It cannot repair unreliable source data or missing ownership. Consequential decisions need human control, audit and an appeal path.”

**“How much will it cost?”**

> “The responsible answer depends on the systems, data quality, number of projects, integrations, security requirements and change effort. We can price a bounded diagnostic first, then estimate the implementation from evidence.”

**“Why should we trust a young team?”**

> “Judge us by the quality of the diagnosis, explicit assumptions, bounded milestones, measurable acceptance criteria, documentation and pilot evidence. We would earn trust progressively.”

**“We want to be like Emaar or Aldar.”**

> “Those are useful references for operating patterns, not templates to copy. We should identify the one JCX problem where the same discipline can create measurable value in the local context.”

## 11. What not to say

- “Your website is bad.”
- “Your team or current vendor made a huge mistake.”
- “Odoo is definitely your ERP.”
- “Your site is insecure” or “you have been hacked.”
- “AI will automate everything.”
- “We can make you Emaar in six months.”
- “This will increase revenue by a specific percentage” without a baseline.
- “We need to replace all your systems.”
- “Lighthouse 59 means you are losing customers.”
- “This startup is the best” based on funding, accelerator membership or a logo.
- “The market research found every PropTech company.”

## 12. Your personal speaking card

### Three ideas to land

1. JCX already has strong visual and portfolio foundations.
2. The next opportunity is a governed, measurable digital front door connected to the operating model.
3. The safest path is one priority workflow, baseline, pilot and scale decision.

### Three questions to ask if you become nervous

1. “What would leadership most want to see changing in twelve months?”
2. “Which process creates the most friction today?”
3. “What is the authoritative source of the data involved?”

### Three phrases that make you sound mature

- “That is a hypothesis from the public experience; your internal data may correct it.”
- “Let us separate identified value, forecast value and realized value.”
- “The website should not become a parallel ERP.”

### Your job in the room

Your senior or co-founder can lead the relationship, strategic context and commercial close. You can add value by bringing evidence, asking product/process/data questions, capturing exact language and reading back what you heard.

Useful handoff:

> “I mapped the customer and operating journeys. Could I share the three observations that seem most important, and then you can add the delivery context?”

## 13. After the meeting

Within 24 hours, write a one-page readback using this structure:

1. What JCX said the problem is, in their words.
2. What outcome and deadline matter.
3. Which assumptions from public research were confirmed or corrected.
4. The highest-friction journey and current baseline.
5. Systems, data and owners.
6. Constraints, vendors and risks.
7. Recommended diagnostic or pilot.
8. Named next actions, owners and dates.

Do not leave the next step as “we will send something.” Agree exactly what will be sent, to whom, by when, and what decision it supports.

## 14. The deeper mental model

The research can be reduced to one chain:

> **A real problem, with a real owner, using a reliable record, through a working workflow, producing a measured result.**

If any link is missing, the project becomes a presentation rather than transformation.

The most credible long-term JCX ambition is not “use the most AI.” It is:

> **JCX becomes Bangladesh’s evidence-led developer: easier to evaluate, easier to buy from, easier to partner land with, easier to finance, easier to monitor and easier to live or work with because important promises connect to governed records and accountable workflows.**

That is the idea you are taking into the room. The meeting is not a performance of certainty. It is the first proof that you can turn ambition into a disciplined, valuable next step.
