# Website launch editorial selection

**Selection version:** 1.1  
**Selection date:** 30 August 2026  
**Corpus cutoff:** 30 August 2026  
**Qualified core at selection:** 295 entities  
**Launch profile count:** 44

## Purpose

This is an editorial launch set for the first public/global analysis experience, not a ranking, awards list, procurement shortlist or investment list. The complete `data/discovery_universe.csv/json` remains searchable as a broad frontier of 978 identities. The 44 records below are chosen because, together, they explain the built-environment technology system across operators, vendors, startups, programs and historical cases.

Every selected profile remains subject to the corpus’s publication gates. A selected record is not automatically publishable: `publication_readiness`, claim review, source dates, identity/status confidence and visibility policy still apply.

## Selection rule

The set was selected using the following constraints:

1. **Analytical coverage:** cover the lifecycle from L1 origination through L12 data/trust, and show multiple technology layers rather than an AI-only market.
2. **Context diversity:** include Bangladesh/South Asia, MENA, East/Southeast Asia, Europe, North America, Australia and other useful comparison contexts without implying market share.
3. **Record-type diversity:** distinguish operating organizations, products, programs/ecosystems and a project; do not count every record as a company.
4. **Operating-model diversity:** include integrated developers, owner/operators, service/data platforms, design-build-operate firms, workflow vendors, physical/energy products, early-stage startups, venture-client programs and historical transitions.
5. **Evidence usefulness:** prefer records with multiple direct or contextual sources, clear workflow relevance, or a particularly instructive limitation/failure. Early-stage and program records are included only when their discovery signal is analytically useful and visibly labeled.
6. **Decision usefulness:** favor records that illuminate a JCX-relevant question: project truth, lead-to-booking, land/site intelligence, construction evidence, procurement, handover, building operations, climate, financing, interoperability or venture-client practice.
7. **Counterweight to hype:** include negative/restructured/acquired examples and records with explicit uncertainty so the launch narrative cannot imply that adoption equals value.

The set does not claim that selected companies are “best.” It is a narrative sample designed to make the corpus’s analytical structure understandable.

## Coverage check

The 44 profiles cover all 12 lifecycle codes through the combined set, with strongest density in L1, L3–L8 and L10–L12. They include organization, nonprofit, product, program/ecosystem and project records; active, pilot, unclear-adjacent, acquired, pivoted and inactive states; and Bangladesh/South Asia, China, MENA, Japan/East Asia, Europe, North America, Australia and global contexts.

The selection intentionally does not contain a standalone JCX entity page. JCX-specific recommendations, public-site observations, Jolshiri hypotheses, ERP/Odoo signals, vendor/account strategy and pilot canvases belong in the permissioned JCX-private chapter until approved for publication.

## Explicit exclusion and deferral rules

The following exclusions apply even when a record is interesting:

- **No default “top companies” list:** funding, employee count, valuation, web traffic, logos, accelerator membership and media attention cannot independently qualify a launch profile.
- **No unresolved identity as a default leader:** discovery identities with shared-domain/name conflicts remain searchable but are excluded from the primary launch profile set until relationship review is complete.
- **No unreviewed MRL inflation:** most current product MRL values are blank; absence of a maturity value is preferable to an inferred score.
- **No product-page invention:** an organization’s category/lifecycle signals do not create a fully evidenced product profile. Under-profiled products remain organization pages or “not yet profiled.”
- **No public-private leakage:** dossier/second-pass findings about JCX systems, current vendors, forms, performance observations, internal hypotheses and meeting talk tracks are not global launch content without approval.
- **No metric aggregation across units:** minutes, dollars, percentages, kWh, adoption, avoided cost and modeled scenarios remain separate case facts.
- **No standards-as-products:** all 39 standards remain a reference layer; implementation and vendor support require separate evidence.

## Editorial handling of the launch set

The launch model should show the 44 as a curated story set while allowing the full 978-record discovery universe to remain searchable. Each profile should expose record type, lifecycle, technology layer, geography/context, current status, relevance tier (if reviewed), MRL (or explicit unknown), claim/source evidence, last observed/reviewed date, caveats and an action label such as analyze, benchmark, pilot, monitor or historical lesson.

Before public publication, claim-level review is still required. In the current corpus, all 834 claims are marked for claim-level editorial review, 130 entities have editorial flags, 22 have tier conflicts and 52 lack verified canonical domains. The launch selection is therefore a prioritization map, not a waiver of those gates.
