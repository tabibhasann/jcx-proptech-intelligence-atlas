# JCX PropTech / ConTech / Real-estate-fintech universe

**Research cut-off:** 30 August 2026 (Asia/Dhaka). **Purpose:** a decision-grade universe for a future JCX intelligence website—not an endorsement list. Companies are included when they are material to a developer/operator, strategically signal a category, or are a credible early-stage company worth testing. The companion CSV is the machine-readable index.

## Executive readout

The built-world technology stack is not one market. It is a chain: **site/title/data → design/BIM → construction/procurement → sales/transaction/finance → rental/property operations → facilities/energy/climate → insurance/security → data and AI infrastructure**. The strongest products win by connecting a system of record to a high-frequency workflow and a measurable economic outcome. “AI for real estate” is now crowded; the scarce assets are proprietary workflow data, distribution through owners/operators, integrations, field adoption, and regulatory trust.

For JCX, the highest-value near-term questions are: (1) can the product create a governed project/unit/customer/vendor record that survives ERP integration, (2) can field teams and channel partners actually use it in Bangladesh, (3) can the pilot show a baseline improvement in lead response, procurement cycle, rework, document readiness, progress assurance, energy, or post-handover service, and (4) can the vendor export JCX data and leave cleanly?

### Tiering

* **Tier 1 — deep profiles:** material benchmark or pilot candidates for a developer like JCX; profile includes product, customer/scale evidence where public, fit, localization, and risks.
* **Tier 2 — watchlist:** meaningful category companies, promising startups, regional options, or specialist technologies that need a specific use case and diligence before contact.
* **Tier 3 — ecosystem index:** investor/accelerator portfolios, adjacent infrastructure, and historical/exited names that widen the map; not a recommendation to buy.

Evidence labels: **A** = official product/company/ecosystem or regulatory filing; **B** = company case study/press release with a named customer; **C** = reputable secondary or investor database; **D** = directory/self-description only. Funding figures are omitted unless a dated primary source is available. Vendor-reported outcomes are not JCX forecasts.

## Company-state and diligence rules for the future website

1. Store `status` as **active / acquired / exited / inactive / unclear**, with `status_checked_at`, `status_source`, and a human note. Never infer “active” from a stale logo or old funding article.
2. Separate **company claim**, **named-customer attribution**, and **independent verification**. A customer logo is not proof of deployment, outcome or current contract.
3. Keep a dated `source_urls[]` array and an `evidence_grade`; every funding, customer, employee-count, valuation or outcome field needs a date.
4. Add `geography_coverage` and `localization_blockers`: title/registry, zoning, payment/credit, tax/VAT, language, connectivity, climate, hardware/service, privacy/security, and regulatory licensing.
5. Score each company on a JCX lens: **problem frequency, measurable value, integration effort, local fit, adoption burden, vendor resilience, data portability, and strategic differentiation**. The output should recommend a pilot or a watch—not “best startup” in the abstract.
