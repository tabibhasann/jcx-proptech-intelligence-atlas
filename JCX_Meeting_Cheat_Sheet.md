# JCX First-Meeting Cheat Sheet

**Use this in the room. Full evidence and sources are in the companion dossier.**  
**Research date:** 29 August 2026

## Your objective

Do not try to sell a website in the first hour. Demonstrate that you understand the larger system, learn what matters internally, and agree on a bounded diagnostic or pilot.

### The three messages to land

1. **JCX already has a premium visual and content foundation.** The business has outgrown a catalogue-style site; it now needs a governed, measurable digital front door.
2. **The website must connect to the ERP and commercial workflow.** Public evidence says JCX is implementing organization-wide ERP, and an older public signal mentions Odoo. Confirm rather than assume.
3. **Become a PropTech leader in stages.** Connect website + CRM + ERP first, digitize priority workflows next, then prove one smart-building use case with a baseline and realized-value measurement.

## Opening

> “Thank you for making the time. I spent some time reviewing JCX’s public portfolio, recent handovers, financing partnerships, and the ERP and IT/MIS direction. I can see that the website sits inside a much broader operating model involving sales, project status, financing, landowners, construction, and customer trust. I have a few initial observations, but I would prefer to validate them with your team before suggesting any solution. Could we begin with what prompted this initiative now and what leadership most wants to change?”

### Your positioning

> “We help connect digital initiatives to measurable outcomes—better-qualified demand, faster response, more reliable information, clearer reporting, and less operational friction. A new website may be part of that, but we would design it around the journeys, data, ownership, and integrations that make it valuable.”

## Facts to remember

- JCX says it works across residential, commercial, and condominium development with Japanese collaboration through Creed Group.
- TBS reported incorporation in 2018, operations from 2019, 12 handovers, and roughly 60 projects at that interview date: 28 ongoing, 22 upcoming, and 10 future.
- The public properties page exposed 64 cards during research; another report referred to 65 ongoing projects. Ask about lifecycle definitions—do not present this as wrongdoing.
- The portfolio is heavily concentrated in Bashundhara R/A and Jalshiri/Jolshiri, with other Dhaka locations.
- JCX leadership has publicly described a move from very large premium apartments toward smaller units and plots in response to market conditions.
- Public partnerships include Prime Bank, United Finance, City Bank, and EBL, among others.
- The official news page is active with handovers, groundbreakings, and agreements.
- The strongest digital clue: a recent IT/MIS vacancy says JCX is implementing ERP across the organization, including integration, migration, BI, cybersecurity, DR, training, and adoption.
- A 2024 public post mentions Odoo. Say: “I saw an Odoo-related public signal; is that still the intended platform?”
- Public values: customer happiness, trust, closeness, uniqueness, integrity, quality, timely delivery, and green living.

## Twelve questions to ask first

1. “What happened—or what opportunity appeared—that made this a priority now?”
2. “If this succeeds in 12 months, what will leadership see changing in the business?”
3. “Which journey creates the most friction today: buyer lead-to-booking, landowner, project reporting, procurement, construction, collections, handover, or service?”
4. “What do buyers and landowners repeatedly ask, wait for, or complain about?”
5. “Which system is the source of truth for project status, inventory, price, approval, customer, payment, and handover data?”
6. “I saw public evidence of an ERP program and an earlier Odoo discussion. What platform and modules are selected, and at what stage?”
7. “When a website, phone, WhatsApp, broker, social, or bank lead arrives, who owns it, how fast must they respond, and how is the outcome recorded?”
8. “How are project facts approved once and kept consistent across the website, brochure, sales, finance, and ERP?”
9. “Which management reports are trusted today, and which still require manual collection?”
10. “What security, privacy, legal, hosting, integration, or current-vendor constraints are fixed?”
11. “Who is the executive sponsor, daily business owner, technical owner, and final approver?”
12. “Which one project or process could prove measurable value within one quarter?”

## Website observations: exact respectful wording

### 1. Buyer form

> “The buyer page appears to reuse landowner-oriented fields such as land size and landowner phone. That may be a simple template issue, but it could also affect conversion and the data your team receives. Where do those submissions land, and how are the fields mapped?”

### 2. Construction status

> “The construction-status feature could become one of JCX’s strongest trust tools. In my public test, it did not provide usable type or location selections. What internal source is intended to power it, and who owns approved update frequency?”

### 3. Project data consistency

> “I saw a few project attributes represented differently across components. With this portfolio size, manual duplication naturally becomes difficult. Which system owns the approved project record, and could the website consume it rather than recreate it?”

### 4. Mobile experience

> “Desktop is visually strong and performed well in the lab. The simulated mobile path was the larger opportunity: roughly 4.3 MB transferred, with an 11.4-second lab LCP. I would validate that against real GA4 and field data before setting priorities.”

### 5. Technology/security baseline

> “A sampled public response exposed PHP 7.4.33, which is an end-of-life branch, and several common security headers were absent from that response. This does not mean there has been a breach; I would ask the hosting team to validate the runtime, patching, WAF, backup, monitoring, and upgrade plan.”

### 6. Measurement

> “GA4 and Meta Pixel are present. I could not see whether call, WhatsApp, form success, site visit, qualified lead, booking, and revenue events are joined to CRM. Is leadership currently able to see source-to-booking performance?”

## If you can mention only one global example

Use the one that matches their problem:

- **Customer portal:** “Emaar One has tens of thousands of users because it supports payments and service, not only property browsing.”
- **Enterprise integration:** “Aldar calls its connected operating model a Digital Spine and reports major adoption and workflow-efficiency improvements.”
- **Pilot discipline:** “CapitaLand runs tightly bounded building pilots with baselines and measured outcomes before scaling.”
- **Realized value:** “Mapletree distinguishes about £88k identified by analytics from about £65k already realized through completed actions.”
- **South Asian starting point:** “Prestige’s CRM, ERP and workflow integration is a nearer first-stage analogy than jumping straight to robots or a full digital twin.”

Always add: “Those are company/case-study outcomes, not a promised JCX return. We would establish JCX’s baseline first.”

## The recommendation in one sentence

> “Build a governed digital front door connected to CRM and ERP, digitize the highest-friction workflows in phases, and then use one measured smart-asset pilot to prove JCX’s PropTech model before scaling.”

## Suggested phase sequence

1. **Align and baseline:** people, process, systems, data, KPI, ERP reality, risks.
2. **Digital front door:** project data, search, correct forms, CRM routing, attribution, mobile performance, security/privacy.
3. **Connected workflows:** inventory/booking/ERP, finance referrals, procurement, project controls, construction reporting, collections.
4. **Customer/landowner portals:** progress, documents, payments, appointments, handover, defects, service.
5. **Smart asset:** one energy/maintenance pilot; measure realized value and scale only if proven.

## Phrases that make you sound mature

- “That is a hypothesis from the public experience; your internal data may correct it.”
- “What is the authoritative source and accountable owner?”
- “How does that show up as a measurable business outcome?”
- “What is already committed, and what remains open for decision?”
- “I would preserve the current investment where it is working.”
- “We should automate the stable path and design human handling for exceptions.”
- “Let us separate identified value, forecast value, and realized value.”
- “A portal earns adoption by completing real tasks.”
- “The website should not become a parallel ERP.”
- “One clean pilot can be more valuable than a broad, unmeasured transformation.”

## Do not say

- “Your website is bad / old-school.”
- “Your team/vendor made a huge mistake.”
- “Odoo is definitely your ERP.”
- “Your site is insecure” or “you have been hacked.”
- “AI will automate everything.”
- “We can make you Emaar in six months.”
- “This will increase revenue by X%” without a baseline.
- “We need to replace all your systems.”
- “Lighthouse 59 means you are losing customers.”

## Objection replies

**“We only need a website.”**  
“We can keep delivery tightly scoped. I would still map project data, lead routing, measurement, content ownership, consent, and ERP/CRM touchpoints so the new site does not become another isolated system.”

**“We already have an agency.”**  
“That can be an advantage. We can support the current team with diagnosis, requirements, integration/data design, measurement, or a bounded workstream.”

**“Send a proposal.”**  
“I can send a responsible proposal after confirming the outcome, owner, systems, scope boundary, timing, and commercial range. Otherwise it would be a guess. A fixed-output discovery proposal may be the right first document.”

**“Can AI solve it?”**  
“AI is strong for approved knowledge search, classification, report drafts, duplicate detection, and assistance. It cannot repair unreliable source data or missing ownership, and sensitive decisions should remain controlled.”

**“Why should we trust a young team?”**  
“Judge us by the quality of the diagnosis, explicit assumptions, bounded milestones, measurable acceptance criteria, documentation, and pilot evidence. We would earn trust progressively.”

## Close

> “My current understanding is that the highest-value opportunity may be connecting project and customer information more reliably across the website, sales, financing, construction, service, and ERP—not simply changing the visual design. I would like to confirm the source of truth, accountable owner, priority journey, and success measures before recommending a solution. Would a short diagnostic or one-project pilot be the right next decision?”

## Do not leave without these six items

- Confirmed problem and desired outcome.
- Executive sponsor and daily owner.
- ERP/CRM platform and implementation stage.
- Priority journey/pilot.
- People/data/systems to access next.
- Named next action, owner, and date.

## Five-minute pre-meeting reset

1. Read the opening once; do not memorize every word.
2. Choose the three website observations most relevant to the attendee.
3. Choose only one global example.
4. Put the twelve questions in front of you.
5. Remind yourself: diagnose first, do not perform certainty.

## Second-pass addendum: the five points that materially change the opportunity

### 1. Separate two ambitions

> “I would separate digitizing JCX itself from investing in PropTech. The first connects the website, project data, leads, ERP, construction, finance, and service. The second should begin with paid pilots on real JCX problems, then consider equity only after repeatable value is proven.”

### 2. Respect the existing ecosystem

- The current website credits Dcastalia, an established digital agency.
- A historical public post mentions Odoo and Kolpolok; confirm whether that platform and partner are current.
- The recent IT/MIS vacancy makes clear that ERP, integration, BI, security, DR, training, and adoption are already an enterprise priority.

Say:

> “If those partners are active, we would prefer a clean multi-vendor model. JCX owns outcomes and data; IT/MIS owns architecture and control; existing partners retain committed scopes; and we add product, journey, integration, measurement, and bounded delivery where there is a gap.”

### 3. Use the Bangladesh benchmark correctly

Shanta, bti, Sheltech, Concord, Navana, Assure, and Edison collectively establish the local baseline: structured project search, lifecycle/status, NRB and landowner routes, Bangla, WhatsApp, video, handover information, and trust proof.

> “JCX does not win by copying a feature checklist. It can lead by making every project fact verified and reusable, every lead and visit traceable, and every update and customer action connected to an accountable workflow.”

### 4. Best first pilot hypothesis

> “My leading hypothesis is a Jolshiri lead-to-booking spine: one project or cluster, all approved lead channels, deduplication, an owner and response SLA, site-visit scheduling, follow-up, trusted project information, and a management funnel. I would validate it with sales and IT before recommending it.”

Ask for the baseline rather than inventing a target:

- enquiry capture rate by channel;
- median and 90th-percentile first human response;
- duplicates, unassigned and overdue leads;
- contact, qualification, appointment, show, booking and loss rates;
- follow-up completion and manual reporting effort.

### 5. Venture client before venture capital

> “Select one problem, invite a small shortlist, run a paid 8–12 week pilot with a baseline, and decide stop or scale. If the product works across at least two JCX projects and has a broader market, investment becomes a separate rational decision.”

Do not recommend a startup investment from public research alone. Product proof, company diligence, valuation, legal structure, conflicts, and investment return are separate tests.

## Your five-to-eight-minute insight segment

1. “Public evidence suggests the ERP program is already substantial—where is it today?”
2. “Local peers set a strong feature baseline; the whitespace is an end-to-end governed lifecycle.”
3. Share two respectful website observations, not six.
4. Offer the Jolshiri pilot as a hypothesis, not a prescription.
5. Explain venture client in one minute only if PropTech investment is discussed.
6. Return to discovery: “Which of these is closest to the problem leadership wants solved?”
