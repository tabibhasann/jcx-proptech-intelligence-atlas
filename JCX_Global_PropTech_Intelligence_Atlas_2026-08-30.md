# JCX Global PropTech Intelligence Atlas

**Research cut-off:** 30 August 2026  
**Purpose:** the evidence-backed editorial and data foundation for a future interactive website  
**Scope:** technology-leading real-estate companies; PropTech, ConTech and adjacent startups; corporate innovation programs; standards; market signals; outcome cases; failures; regional relevance; and implications for JCX  
**Important:** this is an intelligence product, not a procurement endorsement or investment recommendation

## 1. Executive view

### The core conclusion

The frontier of property technology is not a collection of impressive apps. It is the gradual construction of a **digital operating system for the built environment**:

- authoritative land, project, unit, customer, vendor, document, cost, asset and service data;
- workflow systems that connect decisions across the property lifecycle;
- mobile, visual and sensor evidence from the physical world;
- customer and partner interfaces that complete real tasks;
- analytics and AI that can reason over governed data;
- humans, approvals and controls for legal, financial, safety and reputation-critical decisions;
- measured feedback that distinguishes activity from realized value.

The companies producing the most useful lessons fall into five different groups:

1. **Technology-leading real-estate operators** such as Aldar, Emaar, CapitaLand, Prologis, Mitsui Fudosan, Lendlease and JLL, together with Blackstone, Digital Realty, Equinix, Majid Al Futtaim, major homebuilders and hospitality platforms. They show how digital capabilities become part of capital allocation, development, customer experience and operations.
2. **Systems of record, capital decision and execution** such as Autodesk, Procore, Oracle Construction & Engineering, Yardi, MRI, RealPage, ARGUS, Dealpath, Juniper Square, Salesforce, Sell.Do and PlanRadar. They show the importance of durable workflows, controlled project/asset truth, integration and adoption.
3. **Specialist intelligence and physical-workflow companies** such as OpenSpace, Buildots, DroneDeploy, Facilio, BrainBox AI, TestFit, Landeed, Climate X, One Click LCA, Teleo and AUAR. They attack a narrow, valuable problem with data, AI, capture, controls, climate intelligence or hardware.
4. **AI-native early-stage companies**, including a growing YC cohort, that automate maintenance, leasing, transaction paperwork, permits, underwriting, design and construction administration. They reveal the direction of product development but usually have less production evidence.
5. **Corporate innovation and venture ecosystems** such as JLL Spark, LENX, 31VENTURES, BRICKS FUND TOKYO, CapitaLand Sustainability X Challenge, Aldar Scale Up, Hines Global Ventures, Taronga RealTechX, Pi Labs, MetaProp, Fifth Wall, Brick & Mortar Ventures, CEMEX Ventures, RET Ventures, Moderne Ventures and 2150. They show how incumbents discover, pilot, distribute, invest in or absorb technology.

### Twelve findings that should shape the website and JCX strategy

1. **AI adoption is broad but scaled value remains rare.** [JLL's 2025 survey](https://www.jll.com/en-ca/insights/global-real-estate-cre-technology-survey) reports 92% of corporate-real-estate teams running or planning AI pilots, but only 5% achieving most program goals. The future website must make implementation maturity visible.
2. **Data and change capability are the real dividing line.** JLL's [Global Real Estate Outlook 2026](https://www.jll.com/content/dam/jllcom/en/global/documents/reports/research-reports/25-insights-global-real-estate-2026.pdf) reports that 60% of investors lacked a unified real-estate technology strategy, 70% of occupiers lacked an AI change framework, and 50% lacked sufficient digital/AI talent.
3. **Construction remains much less digitized than product demos imply.** The [EIB Investment Report 2025/26](https://www.eib.org/files/publications/20250379-030326-investment-report-2025-en.pdf) reports moderate 3D BIM adoption and very low 4D BIM adoption among European construction companies, with skills, cost, fragmentation and interoperability continuing to constrain scale.
4. **PropTech now extends into construction, infrastructure, energy, climate, industrial IoT and finance.** A narrow property-listing definition misses much of the innovation that can change development economics.
5. **AI-native startups are separating from the wider funding market.** MetaProp and PwC describe renewed confidence and capital concentration where customer urgency, proprietary workflow data and AI leverage meet. [MetaProp's May 2026 confidence summary](https://www.metaprop.com/research) reported investor confidence at 6.5/10 and founder confidence at 8.1/10.
6. **“PropOS” is emerging as a useful design idea, not yet a settled product category.** Agents, digital twins and integration layers may sit above older systems, but data ownership, permissions, model error, interoperability and safe action remain unresolved. See [PwC's 2026 analysis](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/asset-wealth-management/real-estate/emerging-trends-in-real-estate-pwc-uli/trends/proptech-to-propos.html).
7. **Energy and power are becoming property fundamentals.** Data centers, electrification, onsite generation, storage, building controls, demand flexibility and energy cost increasingly affect location, asset value and resilience.
8. **The strongest corporate innovation model for JCX is venture client, not venture capital.** Give a startup a real problem, paid pilot, users, data, baseline and production decision. Investment becomes a separate decision after repeatable value.
9. **Funding is not product evidence.** Katerra, View, WeWork, Zillow Offers and other cases show how capital, technology and market attention can coexist with failed unit economics, balance-sheet exposure or commercialization.
10. **Startup failure and technology failure are different.** Veev's standalone structure did not survive, but its technology continued under Lennar. The website must preserve acquisitions, pivots and strategic absorption rather than marking every status change simply “failed.”
11. **South Asia, MENA and Southeast Asia are not secondary footnotes.** These markets contain highly relevant solutions for developer CRM, title data, full-stack transactions, mortgage, construction procurement, building operations and venture-client programs.
12. **JCX's strongest opportunity is to connect trust with operations.** A governed project truth layer, traceable lead-to-booking workflow, verified progress/handover, NRB service, landowner/JV workflow, finance readiness and post-handover care could become both internal capability and the source of future PropTech products.

## 2. What “comprehensive” means

No honest public research can claim to contain every company in a category whose boundaries and company statuses change every week. This atlas uses a defensible alternative:

- a broad, tiered universe drawn from global operators, public filings, customer cases, YC and specialist accelerators/VCs;
- explicit lifecycle and technology taxonomy;
- current status and historical transitions;
- inclusion based on materiality, evidence or strategic signal;
- deep profiles for companies that can change a JCX decision;
- a broader machine-readable index for discovery;
- claim-level attribution and evidence grades;
- a published correction and refresh method.

The result should be comprehensive enough to answer a decision, not merely large enough to look impressive.

### Record types

The future system should separate:

| Record | Why it is separate |
|---|---|
| Organization | Legal/operating identity, ownership, geography and status. |
| Product | A company may offer several products with different buyers, maturity and risks. |
| Case study | One deployment is not evidence that every customer achieves the same outcome. |
| Claim | Each material statement needs its own source, date, grade and limitation. |
| Relationship | Investment, accelerator, acquisition, pilot, customer, integration and partnership are different relationships. |
| Program/ecosystem | Funds, accelerators, challenge programs and standards bodies shape the market but are not products. |
| Standard/framework | Interoperability and reporting standards need versions and implementation evidence. |

### Evidence model

| Source grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| S1 | Filing, regulator, standard, signed customer/procurement evidence or authoritative documentation. |
| S2 | Company site, press release, vendor/customer case study or accelerator profile. |
| S3 | Reputable independent research or reporting with a clear method. |
| S4 | Structured directory/database requiring verification. |
| S5 | Social, review or listicle signal used only for discovery. |

Claims are separately labeled verified, corroborated, company/customer-reported, atlas inference or unverified signal. A source can be authoritative about a company's own announcement without independently proving the claimed outcome.

### Technology layers

1. **Systems of record:** ERP, CRM, accounting, lease, project controls, IWMS/CMMS and documents.
2. **Applications and workflow:** bounded user-facing work such as inspections, visits, RFQs, handover and service.
3. **Data and intelligence:** integration, graphs, analytics, geospatial, simulation, predictive/generative/agentic AI.
4. **Physical and connected assets:** sensors, controls, meters, access, cameras, drones, equipment and robotics.
5. **Immersive and visualization:** BIM viewers, digital twins, virtual tours, VR/AR and computer vision.
6. **Market and financial rails:** marketplaces, mortgage, payment, escrow, signature, identity and transaction infrastructure.
7. **Enabling infrastructure and governance:** APIs, cloud, security, privacy, standards, assurance and implementation.

This two-dimensional structure prevents a common analytical mistake. “AI,” “digital twin,” or “IoT” says how a product works; it does not say which workflow, buyer or economic result it changes.

## 4. The technology landscape in 2026

### 4.1 From copilots to bounded agents

The newest startup wave is moving from search and draft assistance toward agents that coordinate maintenance, leasing, permit work, transaction documents, construction finance or administrative tasks. The strongest near-term cases share:

- high-volume, repetitive communications or documents;
- structured systems that can provide ground truth;
- a measurable service or cycle-time outcome;
- clear permissions and human escalation;
- an audit trail and reversible action.

The riskiest cases involve consequential pricing, credit, title, legal, safety, allocation or payment decisions with weak data or no appeal path.

### 4.2 Spatial and physical evidence

Reality capture, computer vision, drones, laser scanning and digital twins are becoming practical evidence systems rather than only visualization. They can improve remote review, progress documentation, issue detection, handover and asset records. However, value depends on capture discipline, plan/BIM quality, privacy, retention, data export and an operational process that acts on findings.

### 4.3 Construction robotics and industrialization

Robotic layout, drywall finishing, rebar tying, autonomous equipment, 3D printing, prefabrication and automated manufacturing attack labor, safety, speed and quality. Their readiness cannot be judged like SaaS. Important variables include site variability, utilization, maintenance, logistics, operator training, warranty, code/inspection approval, capital intensity and demand stability.

### 4.4 Data infrastructure before intelligence

Some of the most strategically important companies normalize parcel, title, building, lease, equipment, financial, utility or project information. Their user interface may look less dramatic than a generative-AI demo, but they enable safer automation and portfolio intelligence.

For JCX, the equivalent foundation is a governed record for each project, unit, lead/customer, land opportunity, vendor, contract, payment, progress milestone, handover and service case.

### 4.5 Full-stack transaction and service platforms

Marketplaces are extending beyond discovery into financing, title/document checks, broker workflow, renovation, rental management and post-sale service. This can increase customer value and monetization but also introduces licensing, consumer protection, balance-sheet, service-quality and conflict risks.

### 4.6 Climate, energy and resilience

The strongest property-climate technologies connect measurement to action:

- meters, BMS and equipment data;
- commissioning and fault detection;
- HVAC/control optimization;
- rooftop solar, storage, microgrids and demand response;
- embodied carbon and material selection;
- climate hazard and insurance data;
- water/leak/heat/air-quality management;
- audit-ready portfolio reporting.

No AI energy model can compensate for missing meters, inaccurate BMS points or an operator who cannot implement changes.

### 4.7 Corporate venture and market access

Built-environment startups have a special constraint: they need projects, buildings, users, operational data, hardware access and credible references. This makes real-estate owners and contractors unusually powerful venture clients. The best programs connect startups with actual deployment owners and production decisions; weak programs end with pitch events and no procurement path.

## 5. How the leading real-estate companies use technology

The most valuable corporate comparators are not always the companies with the most futuristic demonstrations. They are the firms that connect technology to a repeatable portfolio, operating process, customer relationship or investment engine.

### 5.3 Corporate innovation models compared

| Model | Exemplars | Appropriate when | JCX position now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal product/platform | Aldar Live, Emaar Hawkeye/Vyom, CapitaStar, Lendlease OLi | A workflow or data asset differentiates the core business and repeats across projects. | Build only the localized layers that create durable advantage. |
| Paid venture-client pilot | CapitaLand Sustainability X Challenge, Aldar Scale Up | A defined problem can be tested on a real project in 8–12 weeks. | **Best immediate model.** Set a baseline, owner, budget and production decision. |
| Corporate venture capital | JLL Spark, Prologis Ventures, LENX, BRICKS FUND TOKYO | The company already has adoption capacity, strategic distribution and investment governance. | Too early as a primary strategy; investment cannot substitute for adoption. |
| Venture studio/spin-out | Hines Global Ventures | An internally validated workflow has a market beyond the parent. | Revisit after the same product succeeds on multiple JCX projects. |
| External accelerator/fund partnership | Pi Labs, MetaProp, Fifth Wall, Taronga RealTechX, NAR REACH | The company wants broader sourcing and diligence without building a fund. | Useful for startup discovery once JCX publishes clear pilot briefs. |
| Standards/certification route | WiredScore/SmartScore, building and data standards | Buyers and investors value comparable, auditable capability. | Use selectively for premium assets; do not turn certification into a substitute for performance. |

The central corporate lesson is that the operating model matters more than the innovation label. Technology produces strategic advantage when the company can supply a repeated problem, authoritative data, empowered users, a deployment route and a decision to scale or stop.

### 7.3 How to use startup cohorts responsibly

The website should never display an “accelerator badge score” as if it were a customer-result score. Each startup should move through a four-stage funnel:

1. **Discovered:** official cohort or credible portfolio confirms identity.
2. **Verified:** current status, product, legal entity, ownership and geography are checked.
3. **Evidence-qualified:** named customers, use context, limitations and at least one decision-relevant outcome are documented.
4. **JCX-qualified:** localization, integration, security, implementation capacity and a measurable pilot design pass review.

### 8.5 Cross-regional conclusions

1. **Workflow precedes marketplace.** Daily operational use creates reliable data; a portal without verified supply is easy to copy.
2. **Data rights create the moat.** Records, transactions, rules, costs, progress and outcomes must be consented, normalized and exportable.
3. **Finance follows evidence.** Procurement credit, mortgage and fractional models should come after identity, title, cash-flow and default controls.
4. **Regulator-connected models scale trust.** Land registry, planning, signatures, investor protection and identity cannot be replaced by a blockchain label.
5. **AI should draft and coordinate before it autonomously decides.** Title, price, credit, allocation, safety and payment require human review, provenance and appeal.
6. **Physical operations decide software value.** Supplier quality, delivery, sensor maintenance, field adoption and customer service are part of the product.

## 9. Market structure and adoption reality in 2026

### 9.1 Adoption is broad; scaled value is rare

[JLL's 2025 Global Real Estate Technology Survey](https://www.jll.com/en-ca/insights/global-real-estate-cre-technology-survey) covered more than 1,000 senior corporate-real-estate decision-makers in 16 markets. It reported that 92% were running or planning AI pilots, yet only 5% said they had achieved most program goals; organizations were pursuing five pilots on average. Data workflows, portfolio optimization and energy management were leading priorities. Sixty-five percent had faced technology-budget pressure over the preceding two years.

This is survey evidence about corporate real estate, not a universal developer result. Its strategic meaning is nevertheless clear: a website that counts pilots or “AI adoption” without showing production maturity will systematically overstate progress.

The associated [JLL Global Real Estate Outlook 2026](https://www.jll.com/content/dam/jllcom/en/global/documents/reports/research-reports/25-insights-global-real-estate-2026.pdf) reported that 60% of surveyed investors lacked a unified real-estate technology strategy, 70% of occupiers lacked an AI change framework and 50% lacked sufficient digital/AI talent. Data fragmentation and change capability—not access to models—remain the bottleneck.

### 9.2 Construction is less digitized than its demonstrations suggest

The [European Investment Bank Investment Report 2025/26](https://www.eib.org/files/publications/20250379-030326-investment-report-2025-en.pdf) cites 29% use of 3D BIM and only 6% use of 4D BIM among European construction firms. Its wider analysis keeps overall advanced-digital-tool use below 30%, although the share using multiple technologies has grown. Cost, skills, fragmentation, interoperability and inconsistent client demand continue to limit scale.

JCX cannot skip the disciplines between paper and AI:

- common project, cost and work breakdown structures;
- controlled names, versions, status codes and approvals;
- model/document ownership;
- field-capable, preferably offline workflows;
- consultant and contractor onboarding;
- open export and integration;
- adoption, exception and outcome measurement.

### 9.3 Capital is moving toward AI, physical workflow, energy and infrastructure

The investor-produced [Nymbl/Klymb Built Environment 2026 report](https://reports.nymblventures.com/built-environment-2026/) says its proprietary database tracks more than 2,100 companies and 4,000 deals, including 494 active PropTech, 809 ConTech and 658 infrastructure-technology companies as of Q1 2026. It reports $22.3bn raised by tracked PropTech and $28.4bn by ConTech from 2021 through Q1 2026, with 61% of 2025 built-environment venture capital involving corporate venture capital. These are the investor's classifications and calculations, not an independent market census.

The useful signal is not the exact market size. It is the convergence of property with:

- construction software, machinery, robotics and industrialized delivery;
- energy, power, storage and data-center infrastructure;
- climate mitigation, adaptation and insurance;
- financial operations and transaction rails;
- corporate owners that can provide distribution and real deployment sites.

[MetaProp's May 2026 confidence summary](https://www.metaprop.com/research) reported investor confidence at 6.5/10, founder confidence at 8.1/10, 63% of surveyed investors expecting to maintain pace and 56% expecting more M&A. This is respondent sentiment, not realized investment. [PwC/ULI's Europe 2026 survey](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/industries/financial-services/real-estate/emerging-trends-real-estate/europe.html) reported 75% using AI/machine learning, up from 51% previously; again, self-reported use is not scaled value.

[CEMEX Ventures' 2026 Top 50 release](https://www.cemexventures.com/cemex-ventures-top-50-2026/) reports approximately $6.57bn of 2025 ConTech investment across nearly 337 deals and says enhanced-productivity solutions accounted for 64% of deals. This is an investor/CVC-produced market classification, not an independently audited census, but it supports the same strategic shift: capital is becoming more selective around industrial viability, integration and measurable execution.

### 9.4 Seven shifts for the website to track

1. **Systems of record becoming systems of bounded action.** Agents read, draft, route and sometimes execute work, but safe action depends on permissions, source data and escalation.
2. **Data infrastructure becoming the competitive layer.** Parcel, equipment, lease, project, utility and identity normalization enable useful intelligence.
3. **Power and real estate converging.** Grid access, generation, storage, electrification, heat and data-center demand affect site and asset economics.
4. **Construction technology entering physical production.** Reality capture, sensors, robotics and industrialized systems face utilization and service constraints beyond SaaS.
5. **Portals becoming full-stack journeys.** Search expands into brokerage, finance, title, renovation, management and service—adding both revenue and risk.
6. **Venture-client access becoming as important as capital.** Real projects, data, users and references are scarce startup resources.
7. **Consolidation becoming normal.** Ownership, product and status changes must be first-class data, not editorial cleanup.

### 9.5 The correct maturity ladder

Every technology claim should be located on this ladder:

| Level | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Announced | Dated strategy, product or partnership statement. |
| Prototype | Demonstrable function in a bounded test environment. |
| Pilot | Named problem, project/site, users, period and success criteria. |
| Production | Live operational workflow with accountable owner and support. |
| Portfolio scale | Repeated deployment across multiple projects/assets with adoption data. |
| Realized value | Baseline, measured outcome, period and implementation cost. |
| Verified value | Credible customer, filing, assurance or independent evidence with limitations disclosed. |

Funding, employee count, media attention and accelerator membership do not move a company up this ladder by themselves.

Every result card should retain:

## 11. Failure, restructuring and technology-survival lessons

### 11.1 Zillow Offers: model error became inventory exposure

Zillow's [2021 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764022000013/z-20211231.htm) records the Offers wind-down and $407.9m inventory write-down. Forecasting uncertainty interacted with home acquisition, renovation, capacity, market and capital risk.

**Control:** limit balance-sheet exposure; stress test price and hold-period assumptions; use human underwriting and exception thresholds; define a kill switch before scaling.

### 11.2 WeWork: a digital experience could not repair lease economics

WeWork entered Chapter 11 in 2023 and [emerged in 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1813756/000119312524159419/d803133dex993.htm). Flexible customer commitments sat against much longer fixed lease obligations while the network expanded aggressively.

**Control:** treat utilization, lease duration, downside exposure, asset commitments and capital structure as first-order product metrics.

### 11.3 Katerra: vertical integration multiplied execution risk

Katerra combined design, procurement, factories, technology and project delivery, then filed Chapter 11 in 2021; the [court record](https://cases.stretto.com/public/X174/11705/PLEADINGS/1170505222480000000154.pdf) establishes the restructuring event.

**Control:** do not apply SaaS growth assumptions to factories and projects. Track utilization, working capital, bonding, supply chain, quality, project margin and governance at each layer.

### 11.4 View: building hardware has commercialization physics

Smart-glass company View entered a prepackaged Chapter 11 process and emerged privately in 2024; the [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1811856/000119312524146158/d826528d8k.htm) records cancellation of existing equity interests.

**Control:** evaluate specification cycles, manufacturing yield, installation, warranty, service, sales cost and financing alongside technical performance.

### 11.5 Veev: company survival and technology survival differ

Veev's independent structure did not continue, but Lennar retained the capability and markets [Veev by Lennar](https://www.lennar.com/new-homes/california/san-francisco-bay-area/santa-rosa/veev-by-lennar).

**Control:** store “acquired,” “assets absorbed,” “product continuing,” “brand retired” and “inactive” as different states. A startup outcome does not settle whether its technology is useful.

### 11.6 Sidewalk Labs/Quayside: legitimacy and phasing are product requirements

[Waterfront Toronto's termination notice](https://www.waterfrontoronto.ca/sites/default/files/documents/plan-development-agreement-amendments-and-sidewalk-labs--termination-notice---termination-effective-date---may-17-2020.pdf) records cancellation of the proposed Toronto smart-district agreement in May 2020. Economic conditions were the stated immediate reason, while the wider case also raised questions about scope, public legitimacy and data governance.

**Control:** establish public purpose, consent, governance, data rights, viable scope and staged economics before installing a city-scale technology narrative.

### 11.7 The recurring anti-patterns

- treating a property, construction or hardware business as if it had software margins;
- scaling principal inventory, leases, factories or credit before model and unit economics are stable;
- confusing funding with product evidence;
- using AI over uncontrolled source data;
- announcing a pilot with no production decision;
- requiring every partner to replace its systems before value appears;
- ignoring export, migration and vendor-continuity risk;
- presenting identified or potential savings as realized cash;
- neglecting regulatory legitimacy, worker/resident consent and human appeal.

## 12. Standards, interoperability and governance: the durable layer

The strategic mistake is to postpone interoperability until after vendor selection. Applications will change; JCX's identifiers, definitions, history and evidence must survive them.

### 12.1 Recommended standards stack

| Layer | Standards / frameworks | What they solve | JCX rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project information management | [ISO 19650 family](https://www.iso.org/sectors/building-construction/building-information-modelling) | Roles, information requirements, exchanges, CDE status and lifecycle process. | Create organizational, asset, project and exchange requirements; tie each to a decision and acceptance test. |
| Model exchange | [IFC 4.3 / ISO 16739-1:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/84123.html) | Open BIM model exchange across design, construction and operations. | Require the exact exchange view/properties; validate more than geometry and retain stable IDs. |
| Information requirements | [IDS](https://www.buildingsmart.org/information-delivery-specification-ids-v1-0-is-approved-as-a-final-standard/) | Machine-checkable IFC content requirements. | Issue versioned IDS and validation results at design, procurement, construction and handover gates. |
| Model issues | [BCF](https://technical.buildingsmart.org/standards/bcf/) | Portable issue, viewpoint, comment, status and responsibility records. | Preserve BCF/IFC IDs, evidence, owner, due date and resolution outside email. |
| Shared vocabulary | [bSDD](https://technical.buildingsmart.org/services/bsdd/) and [ISO 23386/23387](https://www.iso.org/standard/85391.html) | Stable classes, properties, units, allowed values, translations and data templates. | Create governed Bengali/English extensions for Bangladesh terms; version every definition. |
| Enterprise real estate | [OSCRE Industry Data Model](https://www.oscre.org/Industry-Data-Model/Introducing-the-Data-Model) | Lease, occupancy, transaction, portfolio, party and investment semantics. | Map rather than force OSCRE into the BIM model; extend only for local workflows. |
| Operational building semantics | [Brick](https://docs.brickschema.org/), [Project Haystack](https://project-haystack.org/), [RealEstateCore](https://github.com/RealEstateCore/rec) | Equipment, points, spaces, feeds, sensors and building relationships. | Select one canonical internal graph through real use cases and maintain explicit crosswalks. |
| Building controls | [BACnet](https://bacnet.org/about-bacnet-standard/), [Modbus](https://www.modbus.org/modbus-specifications), Matter | Device/controls exchange at commercial and residential edges. | Use secure gateways, network segmentation and read-only defaults; a protocol is not semantic truth. |
| GIS/city/sensor | [OGC API Features](https://www.ogc.org/standards/ogcapi-features/), [SensorThings](https://www.ogc.org/standards/sensorthings/), CityGML/CityJSON | Parcels, buildings, hazards, infrastructure, observations and city context. | Preserve source CRS/precision and connect GIS features to stable parcel/project/building IDs. |
| Carbon and transition | [GHG Protocol](https://ghgprotocol.org/corporate-value-chain-scope-3-standard), [RICS WLCA](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/construction-standards/whole-life-carbon-assessment), [CRREM](https://crrem.org/pathways/methodology/), GRESB | Operational/embodied carbon, value-chain boundaries, transition paths and investor reporting. | Store boundary, factor/version, area basis, period, scenario, evidence and assurance; use reporting frameworks as projections, not the master database. |
| Asset/facility management | ISO 55000/55001/55013 and ISO 41001 | Links asset condition, risk, cost, service level, information and organizational outcomes. | A “digital twin” must support maintenance or investment decisions, not only visualization. |
| Security and AI | [ISO/IEC 27001](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001.html), [NIST CSF 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework), IEC 62443, [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) | Corporate/project/OT security and accountable AI management. | Use risk-based release gates, least privilege, immutable logs, vendor controls, human review and manual fallback. |

No single standard spans the lifecycle. IFC is not an ERP; BACnet is not a data dictionary; a cloud-twin runtime is not a neutral model; GRESB is not a building database; and an API is not an information-management process. JCX's advantage would be the governed mappings among them.

### 12.2 Four tests for every “integration” claim

| Test | Acceptance question |
|---|---|
| Syntactic | Can a second system parse and validate the IFC/BCF/IDS/JSON/OpenAPI/OGC output? |
| Semantic | Do “floor area,” “unit available,” “handover date,” “chiller” and “Scope 2” have the same definition, unit and version? |
| Process | Can users complete a round trip—requirement → exchange → issue → approval → handover—without manual re-entry? |
| Operational | Does the connection have identity, authorization, audit, retries, monitoring, offline behavior and an incident owner? |

```text
Source and edge systems
  BIM/CDE | GIS/land | ERP/CRM | finance | documents | BMS/IoT | field capture
        ↓ open adapters, APIs, events and validated bulk exports

        ↓

        ↓
Operational and public projections
```

The canonical entities should include:

- organization, person/party, role and consent;
- land opportunity, parcel, geometry, title document, claim, reviewer and confidence;
- project, site, building, floor, space and saleable/rentable unit;
- model, drawing, specification, document, issue, revision and approval;
- BOQ item, requisition, RFQ, bid, supplier, PO, delivery/GRN, invoice and payment;
- schedule activity, progress observation, inspection, defect, risk and change;
- lead, channel/source, campaign, visit, offer, price authority, booking and collection;
- owner/landowner allocation, JV obligation, statement and approval;
- handover package, asset, system, equipment, meter, sensor, warranty and spare;
- work order, service request, SLA, technician/vendor and cost;
- energy/water observation, tariff, carbon factor, boundary, calculation and assurance;
- source, claim, evidence grade, status transition, review event and correction.

Each entity needs a stable ID, source, effective date, status, owner, version and access classification. Names and spreadsheet row positions are not identifiers.

### 12.4 Non-negotiable vendor/API requirements

- documented REST/GraphQL or standard APIs plus webhooks/events where appropriate;
- bulk export of all customer content, metadata, attachments, audit history and relationships;
- open or documented formats: CSV/JSON, IFC/BCF/IDS, GeoJSON/OGC, and time-series export;
- stable external IDs and mapping tables—not vendor-only internal IDs;
- SSO/MFA, role/attribute access and project/asset/row-level separation;
- encryption, subprocessor list, data location, retention/deletion and no-training terms;
- rate limits, pagination, idempotency, retry/error semantics and sandbox/test environment;
- immutable audit history with actor, timestamp, old/new value and source;
- backup/restore evidence, RPO/RTO, incident notification and support escalation;
- offline/low-bandwidth field behavior and conflict resolution;
- exit assistance and a verified restore/import test before contract renewal.

### 12.5 Bangladesh privacy and AI governance

Bangladesh's National Cyber Security Agency publishes current [acts and rules](https://ncsa.gov.bd/site/page/50cf7d8c-954c-413f-8f23-eee744a1cd8d/Acts-%26-Rules), while the official laws portal publishes the [National Data Governance Ordinance](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1573.html). This atlas is not legal advice. JCX should obtain current Bangladesh counsel before processing NID/passport files, resident/employee data, payments, CCTV/biometrics or precise occupancy traces.

The design standard should already be higher than minimum compliance: purpose limitation, minimization, explicit access, separation of identity from analytics, documented retention/deletion, breach handling and data-subject correction. Any AI release affecting title, price, credit, tenant eligibility, worker monitoring, safety or payment should record purpose, affected people, source/training data, tested limitations, human oversight, appeal, model/version, output log, drift monitoring and kill switch.

### 14.8 Experience ambition, not a visual prescription

The website should feel like a singular, authored journey through the intelligence—not a standard corporate site, dashboard template, consultancy report pasted onto the web or wall of logos. Its craft, motion, pacing and transitions should be capable of Awwwards-level impact while the research remains the protagonist.

This atlas deliberately does **not** prescribe palette, typography, layout, 2D/3D treatment, motion language, page count or interaction pattern. The receiving design model should first understand the evidence, tensions, lifecycle and narrative, then invent an original visual system around them rather than imitate a named site.

Only the trust constraints are fixed: sources and caveats must remain reachable; discovery cannot look like qualification; claims need attribution; private JCX material must stay permissioned; logos are identity rather than evidence; the experience must remain responsive, keyboard-accessible, legible, performant and usable with reduced motion.

## 15. Corpus, methodology and maintenance

### 15.1 Current structured coverage

As generated on 30 August 2026, the machine-readable corpus contains:

| Layer | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Raw qualified-layer input rows | 319 | Rows contributed by corporate, startup, regional, gap, operator, enterprise-platform, specialist-startup and final residual-audit datasets before identity merging. |
| Qualified-core entities | 295 | Curated identities after 24 duplicate rows were merged: 274 organizations, 12 programs/ecosystems, eight product offerings and one project. |
| Status conflicts requiring review | 4 | Bproperty, PropERP, E-Hishabi and NirmanBazaar remain deliberately cautious where “live product” and “unverified/unclear” evidence differ. |
| Full YC discovery profiles | 128 | Complete dated snapshot of the official Real Estate & Construction directory; 100 Active, 27 Acquired and one Public as displayed by YC. |
| Official ecosystem relationships | 683 | Company–ecosystem pairs across 11 specialist families and 20 named portfolio/cohort/program labels; deliberately not deduplicated into an implied company count. |
| Deduplicated discovery identities | 978 | Search-layer union after name-first resolution, domain corroboration, reviewed aliases and explicit separation of unresolved shared-domain relationships. |
| Discovery-only identities | 683 | Search candidates not promoted into the evidence-qualified core. |
| Quantified cases | 44 | Separate outcome/failure records with baseline/problem, intervention, metric, source type, grade and caveat. |
| Standards/frameworks | 39 | Interoperability, data, controls, ESG, security, privacy and AI-governance records. |
| Claim/interpretation records | 834 | Source-row claims and JCX interpretations with separate provisional attribution grades and mandatory review state. |
| Source-field assertions | 3,896 | Competing identity, status, taxonomy, tier and maturity observations preserved with lineage instead of merged public scalars. |
| Qualified-core relationships | 174 | Reviewed cohort/ecosystem links carried by qualified entities; generic “Independent” provenance is deliberately not emitted as membership. Discovery-index relationships remain in their separate 683-row table. |
| Exact observed source URLs | 1,625 | URL variants found across the scoped research-source and narrative corpus. |
| Normalized source identities | 1,600 | Canonicalized URL keys after conservative normalization; all retain observed forms and document/record usage. |

These are corpus counts, not estimates of the entire market, and the layers overlap. “295 + 128 + 683” is not a company total: 295 is a typed, qualified entity core; 128 is a complete YC directory snapshot; 683 is a set of ecosystem membership pairs. The 978-record union is the correct broad search layer, but it still contains typed programs/products and discovery-only candidates rather than 978 equally verified companies. A record is included deeply when it can change a JCX decision; a directory logo alone does not become a leader profile.

Independent QA found that the first-generation merged master was structurally consistent but semantically unsafe for public filtering: incompatible legacy evidence vocabularies, multi-valued scalars, mixed entity types, incomplete narrative-source coverage and a domain-first discovery join that overcollapsed distinct companies. The normalized bridge fixes the representation—not the underlying research uncertainty—by separating record types, leaving 22 mixed relevance tiers unresolved, removing directory profiles from canonical-domain fields, retaining 52 canonical-domain review items, splitting source quality from claim attribution, expanding the source register to the full scoped corpus, and placing 26 shared-domain/name-conflict identities into relationship review instead of forcing a merge. Product-level MRL remains unassigned until deployment evidence exists. These open review states are a quality feature, not missing polish.

### 15.2 Relevance tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Core: must analyze for a current JCX strategy, architecture or pilot decision. |
| B | Strategic: important benchmark, partner or near-term watch. |
| C | Emerging: strong signal but insufficient maturity/evidence/local fit. |
| D | Context: useful for market completeness or adjacency. |
| H | Historical: acquisition, restructuring, failure or retired product with a material lesson. |

Tier is not quality. A D-tier global platform can be excellent but irrelevant to the current decision; an A-tier local startup can be high-fit but low-evidence.

### 15.3 Market-readiness scale

| MRL | Definition |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Concept/research signal. |
| 1 | Prototype or early product. |
| 2 | Paid pilot or early named customers. |
| 3 | Repeatable deployment in one/few markets. |
| 4 | Multi-market or portfolio-scale production. |
| 5 | Durable enterprise/infrastructure category with mature support. |

The score must be tied to a dated product and geography. A company can be MRL 4 in US mortgage closing and MRL 0 for Bangladesh.

### 15.4 JCX relevance scoring

For a shortlist, score each use case/product—not the company brand—using:

- problem fit: 20%;
- lifecycle leverage: 10%;
- Bangladesh/localization fit: 15%;
- integration and data fit: 15%;
- strength of outcome evidence: 15%;
- readiness/vendor resilience: 10%;
- economics/payback: 10%;
- strategic option value: 5%.

Display the dimension values and rationale. Never convert unknown evidence into a neutral middle score; mark it unknown and reduce confidence.

### 15.5 Evidence and claim discipline

| Source grade | Typical source |
|---|---|
| S1 | Filing, court, regulator, standard, government record, signed procurement or authoritative technical documentation. |
| S2 | Company/customer report, official release, product documentation or named case study. |
| S3 | Reputable independent research/reporting with a clear method. |
| S4 | Structured directory/database needing verification. |
| S5 | Social/listicle/review discovery signal. |

Claims are separately labeled verified/corroborated, customer-reported, company/vendor-reported, atlas inference or unverified signal. An S1 filing can establish a company's reported number or legal event without proving that a product caused the outcome. An S2 vendor case can be useful and still earn a weak causal grade.

Earlier research layers used A–D and A1/B2/B3 conventions for different purposes. Those labels are retained only as legacy lineage. They are never automatically translated into S1–S5 or C1–C5, because an “A” in one layer can mean an annual report while an “A” elsewhere can mean a strongly verified identity. The generated claim register assigns only a conservative provisional attribution grade; every material public claim still requires a reviewer and claim–source decision.

### 15.6 Identity and status rules

- store legal entity, brand, product and program separately;
- preserve aliases, former names and acquisition lineage;
- do not infer “active” from a live domain, logo or old cohort page;
- distinguish active, acquired-active, acquired, pilot, unclear, restructured, inactive and historical;
- date every status check and keep its source;
- split same-name companies, such as the two Haven products or Homebase naming collision;
- represent parent–subsidiary/product relationships rather than merging their claims;
- retain conflicting source states until a reviewer resolves them.

### 15.7 Research and refresh process

**Discovery:** official company directories, public filings, standards bodies, regulator/government programs, operator reports, YC, specialist funds/accelerators and credible reporting.

**Qualification:** confirm identity, status, workflow, buyer, region, ownership, product maturity, source and material JCX relevance.

**Extraction:** store one material claim at a time with verbatim meaning, date, unit, denominator, source type and caveat. Do not turn a funding announcement into a product outcome.

**Review cadence:**

- monthly: broken/high-priority sources, new funding/acquisition/shutdown and correction queue;
- quarterly: Tier A/B status, product and case refresh; new YC/fund cohorts; Bangladesh market changes;
- annually: full taxonomy, scoring weights, standards versions, security/privacy/legal review and archived record audit.

High-volatility fields—funding, valuation, customer count, employee count, status and product ownership—need shorter expiration periods than a stable standard definition.

### 15.8 Limitations

- Public evidence is uneven and selection-biased toward successful vendor/customer stories.
- Private deployment, pricing, retention, security and failure data are usually unavailable.
- Global companies may have no Bangladesh implementation or local data coverage.
- Startup status can change between research and publication.
- Directory and ecosystem counts overlap and use different category definitions.
- Company-reported scale, traffic, savings and funding are not independently audited unless explicitly stated.
- Legal, title, lending, securities, privacy, tax, planning, construction and engineering questions require qualified Bangladesh professionals.
- The atlas supports decisions; it is not a procurement endorsement, investment recommendation or guaranteed forecast.

## 16. Closing position

The highest standard in PropTech is not the largest logo collection or the most futuristic AI demonstration. It is the ability to connect a real property problem to authoritative information, accountable people, a working physical process and a measured result.

For the future analysis website, that means every impressive visual should lead to a defensible record, every claim to a source and caveat, every company to a status and workflow, and every recommendation to an explicit JCX decision.

For JCX, it means building the foundation in the right order:

> **governed project truth → connected lead-to-booking journey → land/JV and delivery workflows → customer and handover trust → operating/energy intelligence → bounded AI and venture-client leadership.**

That sequence is less theatrical than “automate everything,” but it is how JCX can genuinely become a PropTech pioneer rather than merely look like one.
